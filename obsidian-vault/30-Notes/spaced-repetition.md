# Spaced Repetition

## Overview

Reviewing something at growing intervals — a day later, then three days, then a week, then a month — instead of cramming it all at once. It is one of the most consistently supported findings in the study of memory, and it is almost entirely ignored, because it feels less productive than rereading while working substantially better.

## Key concepts

**Forgetting is fast and predictable.** Most of what you learn today is gone within days if nothing touches it again. The curve is steep at first and then flattens, which is exactly why the first review should come quickly and later ones can be spread far apart.

**Each review flattens the curve.** Recalling something resets the decay and slows it, so the same fact needs less and less attention over time. Ten minutes spread across a month beats an hour in one evening, and it isn't close.

**Recall, not review.** The active part matters more than the spacing. Closing the note and trying to say the answer out loud does something that rereading the note does not. Retrieving a memory strengthens it; recognising a page you've seen before mostly strengthens your confidence.

**Difficulty is the point.** A review that feels effortless was scheduled too early and taught you little. A slight struggle to recall is the signal that the interval is right, which makes this method permanently unsatisfying to use — it is designed to feel harder than the thing that doesn't work.

**Why highlighting fails.** Highlighting and rereading produce a strong feeling of familiarity, and familiarity is easily mistaken for knowledge. That mistake is the reason people are surprised by exams they felt prepared for.

## Practical application

The low-effort version, which is what I actually run: when a note is written, schedule one review the next day and one a week later. After that, revisit it whenever it comes up naturally through a link from something else — the linked structure described in [[note-taking-methods]] does a surprising amount of scheduling by itself.

The quiz method from [[learning]] fits here neatly. Hand a note to an AI assistant, ask for ten questions on it, answer them without looking. That converts a passive reread into genuine recall for almost no setup cost, which is the difference between a method used and a method admired.
