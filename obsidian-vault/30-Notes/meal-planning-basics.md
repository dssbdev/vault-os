# Meal Planning Basics

## Overview

Meal planning is the practice of deciding what you will eat before you are hungry. That is the entire idea. Everything else is technique for making the decision stick — because the version of you standing in a shop at 18:30 is not the same person who thought about nutrition on Saturday morning.

## Key concepts

**Plan the week, shop once.** Every additional trip is an opening for improvisation, and improvisation at the shop means food bought with no meal attached to it. One trip also makes the cost of the week visible in a single number instead of five forgettable ones.

**Anchor meals.** Pick four or five meals you can cook without thinking and rotate them. Novelty is expensive: a new recipe costs attention, unfamiliar ingredients, and a real chance of failure on a weeknight. Keep new recipes for weekends, when a bad outcome is a funny story rather than a crisis.

**Leftovers are a feature, not an accident.** Cooking twice the amount costs almost no extra time and buys a free lunch. Plans that treat leftovers as a deliberate part of the week need fewer distinct meals, and fewer distinct meals is what makes the week survivable.

**Every plan needs a fallback.** One meal in the week that takes ten minutes from cupboard ingredients, deliberately placed on the evening you expect to be worst. Without it, one bad day takes the whole plan with it.

**Shop from the plan, not from the fridge.** Write the list by walking through the meals in order. A list built from the meals covers what you need; a list built from staring into the fridge covers what you noticed.

## Practical application

Saturday: choose five meals, one of them the fallback, at least two producing leftovers. Sunday: write the list by walking the plan meal by meal, then shop once. During the week: cook the fixed order, and move a meal rather than abandoning it when the day goes wrong.

The measurable sign it's working is not variety or nutrition. It's the number of unplanned shop trips per week. When that's zero, everything else follows.
