# Digital Life

Where my files live, what happens to them over time, and what I pay every month for the privilege. An ongoing area — machines get replaced, services change their terms, and the pile of photos only ever grows.

## Where things live

One laptop does everything. Photos come off the phone onto it every few weeks, documents get scanned and filed there, and the notes vault sits in a folder alongside them. Everything of value is in one of three folders, which is either admirable simplicity or a single point of failure depending on how charitable you're feeling. It is the second one, which is why [[home-backup-plan]] exists.

## File habits that have held up

Dated folder names in `YYYY-MM` form, so sorting by name and sorting by time are the same operation. Descriptive filenames instead of `scan_0043`, because the two seconds spent naming a file is repaid the first time I need it in a hurry. Plain formats wherever the choice exists, for the reasons in [[plain-text-files]].

## Subscriptions

Reviewed twice a year, in January and July. The July review found two services being paid for and not opened in months, which is the normal result — subscriptions are designed to be forgotten. The rule that came out of it: anything not opened in the review month gets cancelled, and if I miss it I can always resubscribe.

## Superseded — cloud sync as the whole plan (until 2026-07-21)

For about two years the plan was "everything is in the cloud folder, so everything is safe." That is not a backup. A synced folder faithfully copies your mistakes: delete a file, or let something quietly corrupt it, and the sync obediently propagates that to every device within seconds. It protects against a dead laptop and nothing else. Kept here rather than deleted, because the reasoning is a useful reminder of how confident I was while being wrong. Replaced by [[backup-rule-321]] and the plan in [[home-backup-plan]]; the decision itself is in [[2026-07-21-backup-target-decision]].
