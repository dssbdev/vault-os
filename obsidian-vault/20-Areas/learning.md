# Learning

How I actually learn things, as opposed to how I used to pretend to. This area has settled enough to be trusted — the methods below have survived a year and several subjects.

## The loop

Read or watch something, write it up in my own words, then review it on a spreading schedule. Three steps, and the middle one is where all the value is. Writing it in my own words is the point at which I find out whether I understood it, and it is reliably uncomfortable, which is the signal that it's working. The reviewing is covered in [[spaced-repetition]] and the note structure in [[note-taking-methods]].

## Rules that stuck

**One note per subject, not one per fact.** Fewer, larger notes that I actually reread beat a hundred fragments I never open again.

**Highlighting is not studying.** It feels productive and produces nothing. If a passage matters, it gets rewritten, not coloured in.

**A note I can't understand cold in six months is not finished.** Future me has no memory of the context, so the note has to carry it.

## Using an AI assistant

This has become a real part of the loop, so it's worth writing down what works and what doesn't.

**What it's good for.** Explaining the same idea three different ways until one lands. Quizzing me on a note I've written — I paste the note, ask for ten questions, and answer them without looking. That's active recall with none of the effort of writing the questions myself, and it exposes the parts I only think I know. It's also good at the dumb-question stage of a new subject, where I don't yet know enough to search properly.

**What it's not for.** Writing the note. If the assistant writes the summary, I get a good summary and learn nothing, because the struggle of putting it in my own words *was* the learning. The note stays mine; the assistant is allowed to argue with it afterwards.

**The verification habit.** Anything specific — a date, a number, a name, a claim that sounds surprisingly tidy — gets checked against a real source before it goes in a note. Confidently wrong is the failure mode, and it doesn't announce itself. Everything else in this vault is written to be trustworthy in six months, and one unchecked fact quietly poisons that.
